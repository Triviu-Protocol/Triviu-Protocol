Every feature carries one seal. The seal is the claim, and the claim is checkable.

One thing this document will not do: describe the target architecture as though it already runs. TRIVIU is between generations. The contracts deployed today are a working, tested system that is being replaced; the economics the interfaces describe have no contract behind them yet. Both facts are stated where they belong, and neither is softened.

§ 1

Cover, version, date and status

This is the first whitepaper published under the merged TRIVIU identity (Site V7, Console V5.5, Labs V6, Design System & Brandbook V1). It supersedes earlier whitepaper material, which described the atomic-arbitrage generation.

Status of the protocol, in one paragraph. A vault architecture is deployed on Polygon and covered by a suite that passes. That generation is being replaced. The product surfaces published alongside this document describe the target protocol — vaults, mandates, strategies, a Commons and a per-micro-operation economic split — and that target has, at the time of writing, no contract implementing its economics. Real capital is locked everywhere, by design and not by accident.

§ 2

Executive summary

TRIVIU organises strategies, vaults and collaboration into one DeFi experience where context, limits and decisions stay legible.

  • A user owns one personal vault. Ownership is set at creation and never

reassigned. IMPLEMENTED VALIDATED

  • A mandate fences the vault: it is born closed and only opens when the owner

arms it. IMPLEMENTED VALIDATED

  • A strategy proposes routes read-only. It never holds keys and never moves

assets. IMPLEMENTED VALIDATED

  • An escape hatch lets the owner withdraw unconditionally, consulting no

engine, fence, operator or governance. IMPLEMENTED VALIDATED

  • A Commons lets authors publish strategies and be paid from the adopting

user's own half of a positive result. PLANNED

  • Every positive eligible result is split 50% protocol / 50% user at the event.

PLANNED

  • Three environments — Demo, Testnet, Real capital — stay separated, and real

capital is locked. IMPLEMENTED in the interfaces

  • Three networks — Polygon, Arbitrum, BNB Chain — are presented; only Polygon

carries a deployment, and that deployment is the prior generation. DEPRECATED

What TRIVIU does not claim. No return, no yield, no guarantee, and no evidence of profitability. Section 28 states, with numbers, what has been measured and failed to clear.

§ 3

The problem

Most DeFi interfaces answer "what happened" and refuse "why". A user sees a balance and a percentage, and cannot see the route that produced them, the cost paid on the way, the rule that rejected an alternative, or whether the number in front of them is measured, estimated or invented.

Three specific failures follow from that:

  1. A simulation reads as an operation. Nothing on screen distinguishes a

preview from a signed transaction until money is gone.

  1. Custody is ambiguous. "Deposit" appears without saying who can withdraw,

under what condition, and what happens if the protocol stops answering.

  1. Numbers arrive without provenance. A figure with no source, no timestamp

and no environment is a claim wearing the costume of a measurement.

TRIVIU's answer is not a better dashboard. It is a rule: state is part of the information. Every number carries where it came from, when, on which network, and under which environment.

§ 4

What TRIVIU is

A non-custodial protocol in which:

  • the owner writes the law (the mandate) and the code keeps it (the vault);
  • a strategy advises and never decides;
  • execution is atomic — the route completes in one transaction or reverts

whole, leaving only gas spent;

  • withdrawal is unconditional and depends on nothing else in the system.

TRIVIU is not a fund, not a custodian, not an advisor, and not a venue. It does not take deposits into a pooled balance and does not promise a return.

§ 5

Origin of the name

TRIVIU comes from trivium, Latin for the meeting of three ways. The name also carries the classical route of learning that turns information into understanding — which is the order this protocol insists on: understand, then experiment, then configure, then act.

translate="no" is not a styling detail. Automatic translation renders TRIVIU as "TRIVIA"; every surface marks the name as untranslatable.

§ 7

Principles

  1. Education before execution. Any screen touching capital shows context,

source and the nature of the data.

  1. The limit is the message. Status, source, timestamp, unit, cost and the

reason for a rejection.

  1. Uncertainty is never dressed in green. Absence of data is not a zero

balance; a stale read is not a current one.

  1. No promise, in any register. Not in copy, not in a chart axis, not in a

share card.

  1. Every claim is checkable, or it carries a seal saying it is not yet.

§ 9

Vaults and ownership

IMPLEMENTED VALIDATED — current generation.

The vault is an ERC-1967 proxy cloned by the factory at a deterministic CREATE2 address. Owner is written into ERC-7201 namespaced storage at creation. Deposits and withdrawals are explicit acts of the owner.

The escape hatch deserves quoting, because it is the whole non-custodial claim in one function:

function withdraw(IERC20 token, uint256 amount, address to) external {
    if (msg.sender != _owner()) revert NotOwner();
    token.safeTransfer(to, amount);
    emit Withdrawn(address(token), to, amount);
}

There is no registry lookup, no pause check, no operator, no governance. If every other layer stops, this path keeps answering. This is why trusting the team is never required — and it is the reason the claim can be checked in twenty lines rather than believed.

§ 11

User-created strategies

PLANNED.

The Console's builder composes a mandate — network, allowed assets, cooldown, allocation cap, slippage, gas, minimum profit, quote age — and saves it to the browser. No contract is published and no strategy is activated. The builder is a specification tool today, not a deployment tool.

§ 12

Commons

PLANNED.

A network where authors publish strategies and readers compare versions and follow authors. Three separations are structural, not cosmetic:

  • Following receives content.
  • Watching observes a strategy.
  • Simulating tests parameters.

None of the three authorises moving an asset. Publishing a strategy grants nobody access to anybody's vault. The Commons needs a backend, authentication and moderation; none exists.

§ 13

Authors, followers and rebates

PLANNED.

An author publishes a mandate. Each vault that adopts it pays the author a share of that vault's own half of a positive result. The rate is visible before adoption. The author never touches anyone's funds to earn.

The protocol's half is not negotiable and never funds the author.

§ 14

Referral and node structure

PLANNED — and this section carries the sharpest warning in the document.

The Console and the Site present a three-level split: 25% / 15% / 10%, remainder to treasury. No contract implements a referral layer. A search of the entire contract tree returns zero matches for referral, author or rebate. This is not "a different percentage from the code" — the mechanism does not exist in code at all.

Those percentages come from the reference model. They hold only once read from a published, audited contract. Until then they are ILLUSTRATIVE wherever they appear, and any surface presenting them as "the protocol's approved rule" is wrong.

§ 15

Micro-operations and PayFee

PLANNED.

A micro-operation is a single execution event with an eligible result. Fees are settled per event, not per period, so nothing accrues silently between statements.

The base of the calculation matters more than the rate. The target model charges on eligible profit. The deployed generation charges on traded volume. Section 21 states that divergence with the code that produces it.

§ 16

Economic formulas

PLANNED — specification for the contracts to meet, not description of code that runs.

For a positive gross eligible result G of a single micro-operation:

P = 0.50 · G                     protocol PayFee, fixed
U = G − P = 0.50 · G             user's gross economic share
A = r · U        with r ∈ [0, 0.50]    author share, community strategies only
U_net = U − A                    what the vault is credited

Three conditions govern the whole model:

  1. G ≤ 0 pays nobody. No PayFee, no author share. A losing operation costs the

user only what the market and the network already took.

  1. The author is paid from U, never from P. The protocol's half is untouchable

and never funds a third party.

  1. r is bounded at 50% of U, which is 25% of G. The user sees r before

adopting.

Decision gate for a route to execute:

PASS ⇔ vault credit ≥ minimum profit
     ∧ amountOut ≥ amountOutMin
     ∧ quoteAge ≤ maximum quote age

§ 20

Multichain synchronisation

PLANNED.

Selecting a network in any current surface changes the visual context and connects no RPC. Real integration requires, per chain: a source, a freshness window, error handling, and cache isolation so one chain's data can never render under another's label.

Until that exists, no surface may display "synchronised". A state without a source, a timestamp and a named network is not a state.

The divergence, stated plainly

The deployed Fees library computes:

function protocolFee(uint256 traded, uint16 feeBps) internal pure returns (uint256) {
    return Math.mulDiv(traded, feeBps, BPS);
}

with ProtocolRegistry.FEE_BPS_MAX = 100.

That is a fee on traded volume, capped at 1%. Section 16 specifies a fee on eligible profit at 50%. Different base, different ceiling — they are not two versions of one rule.

The founder has ratified that the contracts will be replaced. The specification in section 16 is therefore a requirement for the next generation, not a description of the current one. It is recorded here so that the next contract can be checked against it, and so that no reader mistakes the interface for the code.

There is also a gas refund in the deployed generation (Fees.gasRefund, capped at 100 bps of traded and at one unit of the base currency) that no interface models. If the next generation keeps it, section 16 is incomplete. Open.

§ 22

Oracles, sources and data freshness

INTEGRATION_READY for the oracle floor; PLANNED for the rest.

OracleFloorStrategy reads a price floor and rejects a route below it. Beyond that, no surface reads a live source today.

When sources are wired, three things travel with every figure: where it came from, when it was read, and on which network. A quote older than the configured maximum is rejected rather than used — the age is a gate, not a footnote.

§ 23

Guardrails and rejection criteria

IMPLEMENTED VALIDATED for the deployed gates.

A guard may only veto. It cannot approve, cannot enlarge a limit, and cannot substitute for the mandate. Rejections carry a named reason; the mandate's ceilings are checked before routing rather than after.

The mandate is born closed: empty asset list, ceilings at zero, minimum return at zero. Nothing opens until the owner arms it. Each edit produces a new identifiable configuration epoch so that no order leans on stale configuration.

§ 24

Security and the non-custodial model

IMPLEMENTED VALIDATED for withdrawal; open elsewhere.

  • Ownership is set at creation and never reassigned.
  • Withdrawal is unconditional and depends on no other module.
  • Execution is atomic: it completes or reverts whole.
  • Real capital is locked in every interface.

No external audit report is published. The suite passes; that is not an audit. Any surface writing "audited" without a named firm and a dated report is making a claim this document does not support.

§ 25

Privacy

IMPLEMENTED in the interfaces.

A balance-hiding control blanks values across every surface at once. Profile data — name, handle, bio, photo — stays in the browser's local storage and is sent to no server; the photo is accepted only as an inline data:image so a stored value can never become a request to a third party.

On-chain activity is public by construction. TRIVIU does not and cannot make a transaction private, and says so rather than implying otherwise.

§ 26

Governance

PLANNED.

Two boundaries are already structural in code and are not governance's to move:

  • Governance cannot reach the escape hatch. Withdrawal reads no registry.
  • Governance cannot move a user's mandate. The fence belongs to the vault owner.

What governance will decide — parameters, approved implementations, treasury — and by what process, is not yet defined. Stating that is more useful than a diagram of a process nobody has agreed to.

§ 28

Known limitations

This is the section that decides whether the rest of the document can be trusted.

1 · The economics have no contract. Section 21. The interface is the specification; the code is a different rule.

2 · Profitability is not demonstrated — and the distance is measured. An internal measurement study (Polygon PoS, archival RPC, executable prices via getAmountsOut/QuoterV2, end-of-block state) tested the triangular condition across venue and size:

  • The profitability condition held in 0 of 56 measurements. The model had

assumed a 40% hit rate; measured, it was zero.

  • Residual mispricing averaged +0.045 bps and peaked at +0.496 bps, against

the > 3.014 bps needed to clear costs at $250 — short by a factor of 6.07×.

  • The conditional variable for profit size was recorded as not measurable,

because its condition never occurred. Not zero. Not measured.

The honest boundary, repeated from the source: end-of-block reserves measure residual mispricing, after that block's arbitrage has already run. A result near zero is the expected finding, not a surprise. Closing the gap requires intra-block infrastructure, not more end-of-block measurement.

3 · No external audit. Section 24.

4 · No live data anywhere. Section 20. Every figure on every surface is synthetic and sealed as such.

5 · Referral does not exist in code. Section 14.

6 · The Commons has no backend. Section 12.

7 · Canonical fonts are not yet self-hosted. The three declared families fall back to system faces until bundled locally. No surface may fetch them from a CDN.

8 · Contrast reaches AA, not AAA. 14 of 14 text pairs clear WCAG 2.2 AA; 5 of 14 reach AAA. Raising the rest would change brand primitives and needs ratification.
