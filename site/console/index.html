Chain 137 · reads and encodes · sends nothing

The bytes you would be asked to sign — before anyone asks.

Connect a wallet and this page reads the four Triviu contracts on chain 137, then builds the exact calldata each step of the cycle would put in front of your wallet: the four-byte selector, the contract it goes to, every argument decoded, and what the gas costs at the price the network is charging right now. Nothing is sent. The signing step opens in a later wave; this one exists so the bytes are readable before they matter.

This page encodes. It does not sign, and it cannot.

The wallet is touched by exactly three methods, all of which only return something you already control: eth_accounts, eth_requestAccounts and eth_chainId. There is no fourth. That is enforced by an allowlist that throws in /js/console.js, and by a check that fails the build if a write method so much as appears in that file — not by this paragraph.

The reason the send is not here is written down and is not ours to waive: a page that asks for a signature has to pass three gates first — a Content-Security-Policy whose script-src is this domain, no third-party script on the origin, and the 3D library served from here rather than a CDN. Those were closed on 2026-08-12 and have not yet been audited on the public URL. Ordering matters more than speed: signing first and auditing after is how a drainer gets a page to work with.

What the old console said, and what the contracts actually have

An earlier console in this repository walked a user through "Stage 1/3 — registration", "Stage 2/3 — deploying your triad", "Stage 3/3 — the fence", and opened a wallet three times. None of those three transactions existed: the wallet was a simulation inside the page, and the contracts it named — a user registry, a vault and an executor per user, a referral vault — are not among the four that are deployed. The narration was written before the contracts and never reconciled with them.

So this page does the opposite by construction. Every function name and every selector it prints is generated from contracts/out, the artefacts the compiler produced from the code that was deployed, and a check refuses the build if the screen names a function the artefact does not have. The whole external surface of the three contracts with source here is 22 functions, listed at the bottom of this page. If a step below looks smaller than the story you were told, the step is the true size.

The endpoint is a fixed list rather than a free field, and the reason is this origin's Content-Security-Policy: connect-src names these three hosts and the browser blocks the rest. A text field would have accepted a fourth and then failed silently in the browser, which is worse than not offering it.

The flow, and the exact bytes of each step

Pick the asset the cycle opens and closes on, and the volume. Everything below is then encoded exactly as the wallet would receive it — and handed to eth_estimateGas, which runs the call against current state without signing anything. When the estimate reverts, the revert is what is shown, decoded by its four-byte error selector. A revert is a reading, not a failure to read.

Both amounts are scaled by the decimals read from the token itself, with integer arithmetic — no floating point touches a token amount here. A minProfit of zero means the cycle only has to come back whole; the Executor still reverts the entire transaction if it comes back short.

The gate that makes the second step safe to sign, one day

The Executor reverts unless it ends up with more than it started with. In its own terms: the final balance of the asset must be at least the starting balance plus the principal plus minProfit, or the whole transaction reverts and no leg is left exposed. Your principal is pulled at the start and returned at the end of the same transaction; the contract holds nothing of yours between transactions.

The fee follows the same rule and cannot escape it: it is a percentage of the profit, computed after that check passed, capped in bytecode at MAX_FEE_BPS, and it is zero on a revert and zero on a break-even. What it costs you when there is no profit is the gas, which is why the estimate is on this page and not in the small print.

What this page does not do

  • It does not send a transaction. eth_sendTransaction is not in its wallet allowlist, and a check fails the build if the string appears in its script at all.
  • It does not ask for a signature of any kind — no personal_sign, no typed-data signature, no permit.
  • It does not submit the approval it encodes. The bytes are shown; putting them in front of a wallet is the next wave's job, after the gates are audited on the public URL.
  • It does not invent a route. No router is chosen for you, and where a route is missing the calldata says so instead of filling the gap with a plausible one.
  • It does not read a private key, a seed phrase or a password — it has no field for one.
  • It stores nothing: no cookie, no analytics, no server of its own. The address goes only to the endpoint selected in the form.
