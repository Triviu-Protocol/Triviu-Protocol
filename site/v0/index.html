Triviu

the protocol · initiation

Two contracts of yours.
You deploy them. You close them.

You register with the TriviuRegistry, then deploy TriviuVault — the strongbox for your base currency, whose owner is set at construction and can never be changed — and TriviuCerca, the policy contract that limits what the keeper may ask for. Both are yours. The registry creates the vault and steps away; it never owns anything.

Two contracts you do not deploy, because they are ours and shared by everyone: TriviuExecutor, the atomic engine that opens and closes inside one transaction, and TriviuReferralVault, which splits our fee with whoever introduced you. Neither ever holds your capital.

Entering is connecting a wallet. There is no account to create and no password to set.

Execution is gated, and that is deliberate. Until governance opens the gates, executeCycle reverts by design — you can connect, read the chain, create your vault and deposit, but no cycle runs yet. The fee, when live, is feeBps 50 (0.5%) to treasury 0x73e3…Af6E, charged only on realised profit.

Base currency is USDC (native, 6 decimals) at 0x3c49…c359. Governance and treasury sit behind a 2-of-3 Safe; the deployer key holds no admin role after genesis — the chain is the proof, not this page.

TriviuReferralVault named above is not deployed. It is written and audited; it is not on any chain.

Providing liquidity works differently, and you should know before you sign. TriviuLPVault (0xC52BaD280809672D8EC5D1fcF2d7eCa45a2a423E) is a shared router — one contract for everyone, not one deployed for you. The position stays yours: minting names your own wallet as the recipient and this contract is never its owner. But the sentence “the contracts are yours” describes the arbitrage triad, and it does not describe this one.

Approve the exact amount, never “unlimited”. Opening a position needs an ERC-20 allowance to that shared router, and your wallet will offer you an unlimited one because most of them do. The contract itself pulls only the amount you passed and resets every router approval to zero afterwards — but an unlimited allowance lives in your wallet, outside what the contract can control. Approve the amount you are opening with, and nothing more.

operation · sub-accounts

Capital is sliced,
never stacked.

A sub-account is its own vault: its own base currency, its own fence, its own history. The executor of one never reaches the capital of another. Size is bounded on purpose — a large position moves the price against itself, so the strategy runs many small ones instead.

Operating band · 10 to 500 per sub-account

Below 10 the gas eats the trade before it starts. Above 500 the slippage of a single position starts eating the edge it was chasing. The ceiling rises as the sub-account builds a real record — not as a reward, but because a proven size is a measured one.

Opening more than one numbers the names — Main 01, Main 02 — and each one is born with the Meridian strategy already on its fence, so it is ready the moment it is funded. Leave the opening size blank to open them empty and fund them in one go from Liquidity & gas — that is how a round number divides cleanly. Empty the fence at any time and that sub-account opens nothing.

Today this is a transparent, user-controlled escrow: nothing leaves except back to the account that deposited it. The automated path — spending your own reserve to finish a stuck return leg — is a v0.2 item. It touches user funds, so it ships only after it is specified and audited, not before.

strategy · a fence with a name

Nobody here will tell you
which one earns more.

A strategy is not a secret — it is a set of limits: what may be bought, when, how large, how much slippage is tolerated, when to stop for the day. Pick one of the defaults, or build your own in the Fence and save it here. What none of them carries is a promised return. The console measures; it does not forecast.

Why no strategy here shows a return

Every number you will see on these cards was produced by your own session, by running the cycles yourself, and it always carries how many cycles it came from. Until you run the bake-off, each card reads not measured — because that is the truth, and a plausible number in that space would be an invention. Past results of a simulation predict nothing about a market.

operation · provide liquidity

There is no best pool.
There is a best pool for your size.

Every number on this screen came from reading the chain, one Swap event at a time, over seven days — 100% of the blocks in the window, not a sample. Fees are counted only while the position was inside its band, impermanent loss is the real path the pool walked, and the cost of opening, closing and re-centring is already subtracted. Nothing here is projected. Nothing here is a promise.

Read this before the numbers

A return of 77.4% is true at $250 and false at $50,000 — in the same pool, in the same week. That is why this screen asks how much you are bringing before it shows you anything. The ranking below reorders itself completely between sizes: the pool that leads at $500 finishes fifth at $200,000.

And it is one window. Seven days, ending at a fixed block. The same pool measured forty minutes apart gave 49.44% and 51.97% — a 5% relative swing from nothing but the clock. Read every figure here as what a position would have earned in those seven days, never as what one will earn.

Why there is no button on this screen

You have read eight pools, two networks’ worth of gas figures and a ranking that reorders itself with your size — and there is nothing here to click. That is deliberate, and it is not a roadmap note.

The contract that would open a position does not exist. TriviuExecutor has one external function, executeCycle, and it walks an atomic arbitrage cycle. It cannot mint a concentrated position, hold an ERC-721, collect fees over time, or move a band. Providing liquidity is not a setting we have left switched off. It is a different contract, and it has not been written.

A button here would be the one thing this console refuses to do: offer an action the protocol cannot perform. When that contract exists and has been audited, the button appears — and what it says will obey the same rule every number above obeys. It will name the pool, the size, the band and the network you chose. It will not name a return.

The protocol is paid only if the position produced a result, and never if it closed below what went in. The check is price-free: it compares what comes out against what was deposited, token by token, so a fee is never charged on a position that shrank. A position with no deposit on record is charged nothing — an absent record is not a zero.

What this statement does not know: fees collected through a router other than this console are visible on chain and included; anything moved by a contract that does not emit a Collect event is not. Impermanent loss here is the path the pool actually walked between your open block and now — it is a measurement of the past, and it moves.

operation · atomic route

It closes the loop,
or it never happened.

The route leaves the base currency, walks the hops and comes back. If it does not come back above the minimum after gas, the whole transaction is undone — no leg is ever left half-open. You keep the principal; you lose the gas.

Automation

off
Two brakes, and they are not the same one

Automation is this console's engine: it opens cycles for you on an interval, using the sub-account's own fence. Turning it off stops the engine — it does not pause any contract.

Emergency pause is definirPausa(true) on chain, per sub-account. That is the real handbrake: it blocks opening while closing and rescue stay free. It survives this page being closed; the automation switch does not.

The engine skips any sub-account that is paused, has an empty fence or lacks idle balance, and writes the reason below instead of failing quietly. It stops when this page is closed — a loop that spends should not outlive the tab that started it.

protocol · the layer model

One base layer,
many surfaces above it.

Ethereum splits the network into levels to solve scale: L1 is the main chain — security and decentralisation, at a price. L2 is built above it, settles faster and cheaper, and folds the consolidated result back down to L1. Triviu borrows the shape.

L1

The protocol

Audited contracts, one fence rule, one fee rule, one settlement rail. Slow to change on purpose: a base layer that moves easily is not a base layer.

  • Custody never exists — the vault is yours
  • Atomic settlement: it closes or it reverts
  • Every attempt published, failures included
